“Search me, O God, and know my heart; test me and know my anxious thoughts.”Psalm 139:23 (NIV)

Each question appears on its own. When its time is up, a soft chime plays and the next one appears.

Put it on your home screen

What this is for

Notice a negative thought and catch it. Then try two ways of settling it: arguing with it using logic, and talking with Jesus about it. You’ll see which one actually made it lighter.

When to use it

This is a skill for the middle of the storm, when you’re already upset. For deeper healing of a thought that keeps coming back, Immanuel prayer or journaling goes further. As you practice, you’ll find you can start toward peace from many places.

    How to use the sound

    Name the feeling first, then pick a sound. Keep it barely audible, like a soft blanket in the room, not something to listen to. Let it hold the space while you notice where the feeling sits in your body and breathe into it, letting it rise, settle and ease.

    The tone pairings (174 Hz for fear and despair, 396 Hz for shame and guilt, 417 Hz for disgust and feeling stuck, 528 Hz for anger) come from sound-healing tradition. Research hasn’t shown that a particular pitch works on a particular emotion. What helps is a steady, gentle sound while you breathe, so choose whichever one feels best, white noise included.
